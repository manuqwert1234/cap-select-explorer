How each step works

Step 1, floor. The grid is cut into GxG zones (G = largest divisor of 24 with G² ≤ k, at most 6). Each zone keeps its best token by fused score, so every region of the image survives.

Step 2, multiplicity. The top 10% of tokens by saliency are linked when cosine similarity ≥ tau, then each linked group is split into 4-connected blobs on the grid. A group with two or more blobs means one pattern appears in separate places (several copies of an object), so the best token of every blob is kept. A single large object forms one blob and gets nothing extra.

Step 3, fill. The remaining budget goes to the highest fused scores, so exactly k tokens are kept.

Baseline. The similarity merge walks tokens in fused order and drops any token too similar to one already kept. On repeated objects it keeps one copy and drops the rest, which is the failure CAP-Select targets.

Real LLaVA-1.5-7B selections

These are not simulated. The actual LLaVA-1.5-7B ran on a Kaggle T4 GPU on 5 TallyQA test photos, and each map shows the tokens the real selector kept and the answer the real model then gave. Five photos are an illustration, not a statistic: the full results are in the paper.
